# Research applied to the car showcase

The experience remains a live model and text-free at this stage. The eventual product page should explain the car through the imagery, then add concise copy at deliberate resting points. These are design decisions for this project, not claims that the studios use our implementation.

## Primary sources reviewed

- [Utsubo's Vectr case study](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/vectr-ai-startup-branding-case-study): the complete article describes a guided product journey, restrained visual identity, continuous transitions, and a real-time hero. It reports OffscreenCanvas rendering and an optimized Blender-to-GLB pipeline using instancing, Draco, and texture reduction. Its reported Lighthouse scores and asset reductions belong to Vectr; they have not been measured here. Applied here: a persistent car and studio, shared condition state, instanced detail, cached scene lighting, and steady camera compositions during continuous motion. Moving our renderer into a worker or migrating to WebGPU would be a separate architectural project, not an automatic quality improvement.
- [Lusion's studio site](https://lusion.co) describes its combination of design, motion, 3D, and development, and lists both interactive websites and rendered projects, including Porsche: Dream Machine. This distinction matters: a rendered automotive film is not evidence that the same quality runs interactively. [Lusion's WebGL Scroll Sync explanation](https://github.com/lusionltd/WebGL-Scroll-Sync) identifies native-scroll/rAF drift and explains a single-canvas approach, with an absolute-positioned, padded canvas workaround when visuals must align with scrolling DOM elements. Our current fixed full-screen scene has no anchored copy. We retain native scroll; when copy is added to the pinned stage, its transitions should use the same damped progress as the camera. The padded-canvas workaround is only relevant if the design later attaches 3D to independently scrolling document blocks.
- [Utsubo's glTF curve exporter](https://github.com/utsuboco/gltf_curve_exporter) preserves authored Bezier/NURBS control data for Three.js. Applied principle: author the camera path rather than permit free orbit. Our paths use continuous Hermite interpolation, gentle banking, and continuously moving entry/exit compositions. The requested horizontal studio side tracks remain, connected by continuous flights through a wider rear arc. We have not installed the exporter or claimed that these paths came from Blender.
- [Active Theory's ActiveFrame documentation](https://github.com/activetheory/activeframe) describes frame-accurate WebCodecs decoding of encoded video samples and direct use in graphics contexts. It is a useful alternative for interactive films, but the user explicitly prefers the live model. No video decoding pipeline has been introduced. Active Theory's homepage provides little readable material without running its app, so this assessment relies on its published tool documentation rather than inferring proprietary renderer details.
- [Lumette's current public homepage](https://lumette.ai) is reachable as HTML and identifies cinematic 3D website kits, but its initial document does not expose the reel's scene or sufficient rendering detail. The Instagram reel has not been inspected. No attribution of ray tracing, fluid simulation, or a particular 3D pipeline is justified from that document.

## Flow and eventual copy

The latest user direction removes the desert, all chapter-cut fades, and repeating light pulses. The page now follows one visual story: reveal the silhouette, track the dry bodywork, arc around the rear, return through rain, inspect water detail, and continue directly into a thick foam application. Mist and the entire studio persist through the soap finale. Three authored lighting failures add dramatic accents at selected camera moments, with complete blackouts followed by overcorrection and settling; the camera continues through them.

The rain/soap camera join matches position, target, lens, roll, and global velocity. There are no static scroll holds. Future copy should be real HTML driven by the same rendered camera clock, stay clear of the silhouette, and fade away before a lighting failure so the blackout remains intentional. The visual prototype still has no visible copy or controls. Product specifications and the final action need approved wording; no unverified claims have been added.

## Graphics priorities

Material separation, coherent reflection sources, varied particle scales, depth-tested rain, and restrained exposure do more for this model than indiscriminately adding glow. The visible hexagonal fixtures are represented in the reflection environment; water, paint, and froth use different material responses. See [rendering notes](rendering-notes.md) for implementation details and approximations. This is rasterized real-time rendering, not ray tracing or a fluid solver. The original model's geometry and texture quality remain the fidelity ceiling, and software-browser testing cannot establish desktop GPU frame rate.
